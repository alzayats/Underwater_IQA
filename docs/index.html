Marine imaging / Reproducible methods

Measuring what a survey photograph is actually worth.

Modern reef surveys return tens of thousands of images per expedition, and a substantial fraction of them are unusable. UNIQAT scores every frame against 37 reference-free metrics so that the decision to keep or discard an image becomes a number a reader can check, rather than a judgement made once and never written down.

That sentence appears in methods sections across the literature, and it is almost never reproducible. UNIQAT replaces it with a number, and with the nine-category profile behind that number.

What was measured

Evaluated on 130,845 unfiltered survey images

Nine surveys from the AIMS Technology Transformation Program, spanning three reef sectors of the Great Barrier Reef, four research trips and three ReefScan camera configurations. No manual filtering was applied first, so the distribution below is what raw survey data actually looks like.

The depth relationship is the useful check. Nothing in the metrics knows about depth, yet quality falls monotonically with it in all nine surveys, which is what underwater optics predicts.

The metrics

Nine diagnostic categories

Every metric is a closed-form function of the pixel array, computed algorithmically with no learned parameters, so the pipeline is deterministic and needs no training data. A single scalar tells you an image is bad; these tell you why, which is what you need to decide whether to re-acquire, correct, or discard it.

  • 9Colour cast analysis
  • 7Colour distribution
  • 5Contrast measures
  • 5Feature richness
  • 3Sharpness and blur
  • 2Visibility and turbidity
  • 2Underwater indices, UCIQE and UIQM
  • 2Information content
  • 2Composite assessment scores

The suite runs on a laptop and scales to a cluster. Deep learning architectures are included as training-ready code for anyone needing faster single-image inference and willing to train on their own imagery.
