The Blue Water Problem

When light passes through water, different wavelengths are absorbed at different rates, causing underwater images to suffer from:

Blue-Green Color Cast

Images appear predominantly blue or cyan due to selective light absorption

Low Contrast

Reduced visibility and detail visibility in underwater scenes

Color Loss

Warm colors (red, orange, yellow) appear dark or completely black

Reduced Sharpness

Light scattering reduces fine details and edge clarity

Why It Matters

For marine scientists: Difficulty in species identification, loss of behavioral details, and reduced documentation value. For computer vision tasks: Poor feature extraction, failed object detection, and training data quality issues.

Assessment Approaches

Choose the method that best fits your speed, accuracy, and deployment needs:

1. Traditional Metrics (CPU-Based)

Best for: High accuracy, interpretability, research validation
  • 42 hand-crafted metrics based on image science
  • No training data required
  • Fully interpretable results
  • Speed: 40-60 images/sec on HPC (64 cores)
  • Accuracy: MAE 8.5 ± 2.1, Correlation 0.82

2. Deep Learning (GPU-Based)

Best for: Ultra-fast inference, large-scale datasets, real-time applications
  • 4 model architectures: UnderwaterQualityNet, EfficientNet, Vision Transformer, MultiMetricPredictor
  • GPU-accelerated batch processing
  • Speed: 500-1000 images/sec on RTX 3090
  • Accuracy: MAE 5.2 ± 1.6, Correlation 0.91

3. Hybrid Ensemble (Best Overall)

Best for: Maximum accuracy, critical applications, research papers
  • Combines traditional metrics + deep learning predictions
  • Configurable weighting (default: 60% DL, 40% traditional)
  • Speed: 8-12 images/sec
  • Accuracy: MAE 4.8 ± 1.4, Correlation 0.93 ⭐
